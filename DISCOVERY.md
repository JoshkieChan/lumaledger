## 3. Falsifiable hypothesis

**Current hypothesis status: substantially undermined, not validated.** The original energy-assistance follow-through hypothesis was that a LumaLedger checklist would improve seven-day completion of a relevant next step versus the same resource links alone. Opportunity Council's existing Island County service explicitly tracks available programs, helps residents understand program fit, and completes/submits applications. That incumbent overlaps with the proposed workflow, so the hypothesis should not be presented as a differentiated product opportunity. Whether LumaLedger adds measurable value beyond Opportunity Council and existing utility/211 routes is unknown and would require approved research and a suitable comparison.

**Water-assistance concept status:** The household discovery/checklist concept is not a defensible differentiator as currently framed because established operators and software already address discovery, enrollment, verification, and program administration. A narrower hypothesis—that some small utilities have administrative burdens that existing options do not solve—is a candidate for validation only. EPA's national analysis does not establish a local utility's burden, Oak Harbor's eligible or enrolled population, a take-up gap, or household demand. Any later research would first need to establish a specific local problem and compare existing workflows before testing a guide or product.

**Evidence needed before considering a pilot:** Local evidence of a specific problem not already addressed by existing utility workflows, assistance operators, or software; current official coverage and application details for Island County water systems; and Oak Harbor enrollment/eligible-household data if publicly available. EPA, Pennsylvania, Philadelphia, Seattle, and San Antonio evidence is not a substitute for local need or take-up data. Do not claim participation gaps without a local denominator. Any interviews or other outreach require separate explicit user approval; no outreach is authorized by this brief.

## 5. Neutral interview guides

### Household opening and questions

Read the consent and privacy statement first. Participation is voluntary; skipping questions or stopping has no effect on access to assistance. Do not request names, contact details, account numbers, exact addresses, case numbers, documents, or precise eligibility information.

1. “Please tell me about the last time you looked for help with an energy or water bill, starting wherever makes sense to you.”
2. “What did you do next? What happened after that?”
3. “Were there any points where you paused, changed direction, or stopped? What was happening then?”
4. “What information, if any, did you need to decide what to do?”
5. “How did you decide which source or person to trust?”
6. “What would you change about that process, if anything?”
7. After showing the neutral task: “What do you think this page does with the information you enter?”
8. “What would you do next using this page? Please think aloud; I will not guide you unless you ask.”
9. “What, if anything, is unclear or concerning about the checklist or follow-up questions?”
10. “How would you prefer to tell whether a next step was completed, if at all?”
11. “If you have ever looked for help with a water bill, what did you try and what happened next?”

Avoid asking participants to disclose income, ZIP, utility account information, immigration status, benefits, or documents. Use synthetic task values.

### Frontline navigator opening and questions

State that the discussion is exploratory and is not a sales pitch, referral request, or request to share client information. Do not collect client-identifiable examples or organizational confidential information.

1. “Could you describe the steps people generally take when seeking help with an energy bill?”
2. “Where do handoffs between organizations or services happen in that process?”
3. “What tends to make a handoff work well? What tends to make it difficult?”
4. “How do you currently know whether a referral or next step was completed, if you know?”
5. “Which parts of this process are already supported by tools or internal workflows?”
6. “What work, if any, would a checklist like this add or remove?”
7. “What information would be unsafe or inappropriate for a tool like this to collect?”
8. “What would you need to verify before sharing a tool with a household?”
9. “What evidence would make you decide this is not useful?”
10. “Who else, if anyone, should be consulted before a pilot could be considered?”

Do not ask navigators for client names, case histories, referral records, confidential operational data, or introductions/contact details without separate user approval.

## 7. Metrics and decision gates

Pre-register definitions and the comparison method before any approved sessions. Report denominators and missing responses; with a small sample, show counts as well as percentages.

| Metric | Proposed definition |
| --- | --- |
| Task comprehension | Participant can identify the applicable water utility/official help route and explain that utilities/providers determine their own assistance terms and eligibility; a guide or checklist does not submit an application or provide assistance. |
| Action completion | Household reports taking a relevant next step within seven days (for example, checking with their utility); label self-reported and separate from application completion or verified outcomes. |
| Guide effect | Difference in correct identification of a next step between a neutral guide and the same official links alone; any small-sample result is directional, not a powered causal estimate. |
| Referral friction | Observed confusion, dead ends, repeated steps, time to identify a next action, and participant-described barriers. |
| Outcome completeness | Counts and missingness for contacted/applied/received-support and optional bill comparisons; never infer causality. |
| Privacy/usability | Requests to enter sensitive details, privacy misunderstandings, task completion, and perceived burden; target zero sensitive-identifier collection and zero transmission. |
| Navigator fit | Perceived duplication, operational burden, safety concerns, and concrete use cases from navigator interviews. |

**Continue to a separately approved pilot only if** at least 6/8 household participants complete the water-route task without assistance, at least 6/8 correctly understand the utility/provider boundary, no sensitive identifiers are collected or transmitted, and the guide produces a directional improvement of at least 2 of 8 participants in correctly identifying an applicable next step versus the same official links alone. This would justify further testing only; it would not establish real-world need, increased enrollment, or effectiveness.

**Pivot** if comprehension and privacy gates pass but the guide does not meet that directional threshold, or if navigators identify duplication: revise the service question or stop testing the interface before making positioning claims.

**Stop the current concept** if the prototype leads participants to believe it determines eligibility, submits applications, contacts services, or verifies outcomes; if sensitive identifiers are required for the proposed value; if a privacy/safety incident occurs; or if the existing tools already support the identified follow-through need with less burden.

No gate proves willingness to pay, market size, defensibility, or broad impact. Those require separate evidence and approval.
