**Interpretation:** Public evidence confirms that Washington residents already have resource-discovery and official application routes through WA 211, Washington Commerce's LIHEAP provider locator, and utility programs; HHS points people to state offices/PUCs/PSCs for program information. None of this proves that user-facing referral handoffs or application completion are broken, effective, or changing in Washington. No current participation gap or service failure is established.

## 2. Unknowns to resolve

- Which Washington State agencies, community action agencies, tribes, utilities, and referral partners serve each area, and what their current intake, handoff, follow-up, and closed-loop workflows are.
- Which local LIHEAP providers and utilities cover any eventual study area, what their exact appointment/contact routes are, and how their service boundaries relate to counties, tribes, and utility territories. Washington Commerce provides an official provider locator, but its displayed results and provider boundaries must be checked from official sources before choosing a locality.
- What the latest publicly accessible Commerce Section 120 data/reports show at utility level, whether they include measures relevant to referral/application completion, and what gaps remain; the statute's required aggregate measures are not household-level follow-through outcomes.
- Whether WA 211's database and search routes cover a participant's specific need and location, how current listings are, and when telephone navigation differs from online search. WA 211's listing count is a statement on its public page, not an independent completeness or outcomes measure.
- Washington-specific LIHEAP program rules, administering offices, application steps, and service availability for any eventual participant; national HHS pages are starting routes, not confirmation of a Washington household's eligibility or local provider workflow.
- Which counties, tribal service areas, utility territories, languages, and rural/urban contexts should be included for a bounded study; none is selected or inferred yet.
- How often households must move between directories, eligibility guidance, utility contacts, and assistance applications to resolve one affordability need.
- Where people abandon or repeat steps, and what causes that friction (including wait times, documentation, language access, trust, or changing circumstances).
- Whether people find a private, user-controlled cross-service checklist useful beyond existing navigator and referral workflows.
- Whether a checklist improves completed actions against a relevant baseline, for whom, and at what cost or added burden.
- Which measures participants consider safe and useful to report; whether before/after bill comparisons are understandable and comparable.
- Whether navigators see value, duplication, workflow burden, or any need for provider-facing integrations.
- Whether any organization would fund this work, and whether procurement, consent, and data-governance requirements can be met.

An unobserved capability or local workflow is unknown, not absent. Interviews and product-page review cannot by themselves validate impact or willingness to pay.

## 3. Falsifiable hypothesis

**Hypothesis:** Among adults in a Washington State service area selected only after validation and approval who seek help with household energy costs, people using the LumaLedger cross-service checklist will complete at least one relevant next step within seven days at a higher rate than people given the same verified existing-resource links without the checklist.

**Falsification rule:** Do not support the hypothesis if the prototype group does not exceed the link-only comparison on the pre-registered seven-day action-completion measure, or if participants cannot accurately describe that services determine their own eligibility and outcomes are self-reported. A small discovery sample is directional only; it cannot prove general effectiveness or a market gap.

## 5. Neutral interview guides

### Household opening and questions

Read the consent and privacy statement first. Participation is voluntary; skipping questions or stopping has no effect on access to assistance. Do not request names, contact details, account numbers, exact addresses, case numbers, documents, or precise eligibility information.

1. “Please tell me about the last time you looked for help with an energy bill, starting wherever makes sense to you.”
2. “What did you do next? What happened after that?”
3. “Were there any points where you paused, changed direction, or stopped? What was happening then?”
4. “What information, if any, did you need to decide what to do?”
5. “How did you decide which source or person to trust?”
6. “What would you change about that process, if anything?”
7. After showing the neutral task: “What do you think this page does with the information you enter?”
8. “What would you do next using this page? Please think aloud; I will not guide you unless you ask.”
9. “What, if anything, is unclear or concerning about the checklist or follow-up questions?”
10. “How would you prefer to tell whether a next step was completed, if at all?”

Avoid asking participants to disclose income, ZIP, utility account information, immigration status, benefits, or documents. Use synthetic task values.

### Frontline navigator opening and questions

State that the discussion is exploratory and is not a sales pitch, referral request, or request to share client information. Do not collect client-identifiable examples or organizational confidential information.

1. “Could you describe the steps people generally take when seeking help with an energy bill?”
2. “Where do handoffs between organizations or services happen in that process?”
3. “What tends to make a handoff work well? What tends to make it difficult?”
4. “How do you currently know whether a referral or next step was completed, if you know?”
5. “Which parts of this process are already supported by tools or internal workflows?”
6. “What work, if any, would a checklist like this add or remove?”
7. “What information would be unsafe or inappropriate for a tool like this to collect?”
8. “What would you need to verify before sharing a tool with a household?”
9. “What evidence would make you decide this is not useful?”
10. “Who else, if anyone, should be consulted before a pilot could be considered?”

Do not ask navigators for client names, case histories, referral records, confidential operational data, or introductions/contact details without separate user approval.

## 6. Consent, privacy, and safety constraints

- No Washington local interviews, outreach, recruitment, submissions, or participant enrollment have occurred. Obtain explicit user approval before any such activity.
- If approved, explain the purpose, activities, duration, voluntary nature, risks, note-taking, retention/deletion, and that declining does not affect service access. Obtain affirmative informed consent before research activities; do not treat use of this demo as research consent.
- Use synthetic inputs for usability tasks. Do not collect names, contact information, exact address, ZIP, utility/provider account identifiers, case/application numbers, documents, immigration status, or precise benefit/eligibility details.
- Do not record audio/video or link notes to identity unless a separately reviewed protocol and explicit consent authorize it. Default to no recording.
- Keep notes de-identified and minimal; summarize aggregate counts and task observations. Define access and deletion timing before recruitment. Do not copy participant-level bills or outcomes into this repository or prototype.
- Never represent self-reported contact, application, support, or bill changes as verified. A before/after difference is not causal evidence.
- This brief is not legal, IRB, or organizational ethics approval. Obtain required review for the selected setting before contact or data collection.

## 7. Metrics and decision gates

Pre-register definitions and the comparison method before any approved sessions. Report denominators and missing responses; with a small sample, show counts as well as percentages.

| Metric | Proposed definition |
| --- | --- |
| Task comprehension | Participant can explain that 211/LIHEAP services provide their own guidance and determine eligibility; checklist completion itself is not assistance or eligibility. |
| Action completion | Household reports completing at least one relevant next step within seven days; label self-reported and separate from verified outcomes. |
| Checklist effect | Difference in action-completion proportion between checklist and same-links-only tasks; directional signal, not a powered causal estimate. |
| Referral friction | Observed confusion, dead ends, repeated steps, time to identify a next action, and participant-described barriers. |
| Outcome completeness | Counts and missingness for contacted/applied/received-support and optional bill comparisons; never infer causality. |
| Privacy/usability | Requests to enter sensitive details, privacy misunderstandings, task completion, and perceived burden; target zero sensitive-identifier collection and zero transmission. |
| Navigator fit | Perceived duplication, operational burden, safety concerns, and concrete use cases from navigator interviews. |

**Continue to a larger, separately approved pilot only if** at least 6/8 household participants complete the task without assistance, at least 6/8 correctly understand the service/eligibility boundary, no sensitive identifiers are collected or transmitted, and the checklist comparison shows a directional improvement of at least 2 of 8 participants in relevant next-step completion versus links alone. The small sample does not establish effectiveness.

**Pivot** if comprehension and privacy gates pass but the checklist does not meet that directional threshold, or if navigators identify duplication: revise the handoff/checklist concept and test again before making positioning claims.

**Stop the current concept** if the prototype leads participants to believe it determines eligibility, submits applications, contacts services, or verifies outcomes; if sensitive identifiers are required for the proposed value; if a privacy/safety incident occurs; or if the existing tools already support the identified follow-through need with less burden.

No gate proves willingness to pay, market size, defensibility, or broad impact. Those require separate evidence and approval.

This does not establish current listing completeness, any county/provider workflow, or referral/application completion rates.
